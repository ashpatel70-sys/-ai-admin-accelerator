5 AI email prompts a small business can actually reuse.

Copy these starting points for common administrative emails, replace the placeholders with verified facts, and review the final drafts before sending. No sign-up required.

Before you use AI to draft an email

AI can help with structure and tone, but it cannot verify your price, stock, policy or legal obligations for you. Use fictional or anonymised examples when trying out prompts, avoid uploading confidential customer details to external tools, and check every fact and commitment in the draft.

01

Reply to a new customer enquiry

For an enquiry that needs acknowledgement before you've confirmed all the details.

Act as a careful UK customer support assistant. Draft a concise reply using only the verified information below. Customer query: [PASTE NON-SENSITIVE SUMMARY]. Facts I can confirm: [DETAILS]. Information I still need: [QUESTIONS]. Tone: helpful, professional British English. Do not invent pricing, delivery dates, policies or availability. Write a subject line and an email under 140 words. End with a clear next action. Flag any missing facts separately.
02

Follow up after sending a proposal

Nudge a prospect courteously without suggesting urgency that isn't real.

Write a polite, low-pressure follow-up email for a UK business prospect. We sent a proposal about [SERVICE] on [DATE]. The main benefit discussed was [SPECIFIC BUSINESS BENEFIT]. Our next available step is [VERIFIED STEP]. Use British English and a direct subject line. Ask whether they have questions or would like to discuss next steps. Do not claim limited availability, a discount, or a deadline unless explicitly stated. Maximum 120 words.
03

Turn meeting notes into an action email

Create a short recap from a verified set of points.

Convert these meeting notes into a professional follow-up email with (1) decisions taken, (2) action items, (3) named owners only if specified, and (4) due dates only if stated. Notes: [REDACTED MEETING NOTES]. Do not assume an agreement was reached where the notes are unclear. Put anything unconfirmed under "To confirm". Use British English. Keep a friendly, factual tone and make the actions easy to scan.
04

Respond to a customer complaint

Acknowledge the concern while avoiding unsupported admissions or promises.

Draft a respectful UK customer service response to this complaint summary: [NON-SENSITIVE SUMMARY]. Verified facts: [FACTS]. What we can offer: [APPROVED OPTIONS]. Our formal complaints process or response timeframe: [VERIFIED POLICY]. Acknowledge the customer's concern, explain the next step and avoid blaming them. Do not promise a refund, cite legislation, or admit fault unless explicitly authorised. Use plain British English and keep the draft under 170 words.
05

Request missing onboarding information

Help a new client complete an agreed brief efficiently.

Write a friendly onboarding email for a newly confirmed UK business client. Service purchased: [SERVICE]. Confirmed deliverables: [SCOPE]. We need these items to begin: [NON-SENSITIVE INFORMATION LIST]. Secure submission channel: [APPROVED CHANNEL]. Confirmed schedule or expected next update: [VERIFIED TIMING]. Explain why each item is needed and what happens after receipt. Never ask for passwords, full card details or sensitive personal data by email. Use a short bulleted list, British English and a welcoming professional tone.

Check the email before pressing Send

  • Confirm any prices, deadlines, stock levels, identities and names against your own records.
  • Remove AI placeholders and unsupported claims.
  • Check spelling, tone, personal data and accessibility.
  • Ensure any cancellation, refund or legal statements match your approved policies.
  • Keep human approval for sensitive replies and financial commitments.

These prompts provide drafting support, not legal or regulated advice.
